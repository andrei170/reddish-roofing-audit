The Finding That Matters Most

You have 46 Google reviews at 4.4 stars. Your website shows none of them.

Customers name you personally and keep saying the same two things — the price and the workmanship. "Fabulous value for money and talked through any changes needed". "Great price and great guy." "Excellent price also."

Not one of those words appears on reddishroofing.co.uk. The site has no reviews, no rating, no testimonials and no link to the Google profile. Someone comparing three roofers finds nothing on your site to verify, then leaves.

You are also on one platform only. No Checkatrade, TrustATrader, Trustpilot, MyBuilder, Rated People, Bark, Yell or Freeindex profile exists. Forty-six reviews is a real asset sitting in a single basket, invisible from your own front door.

You are paying for stock photos of somebody else's roofs.

Uploaded in February this year are five photographs of your own finished work: a slate roof with dry verge and dormers, a clay plain tile hip with a lead valley, a slate roof with velux and full leadwork, a mock-Tudor gable with new bargeboards, and a completed rear elevation. They are genuinely good. Better than most roofing companies in Greater Manchester have.

Sitting beside them, from the original 2022 site build, are stock library images. One is a bearded model in a yellow hard hat drilling into metal profile sheeting — an industrial covering, on a company whose actual work is slate and clay tile on Stockport houses. Others are generic template placeholders named 4, 18, 19, 21, 24, 26 and mission-1.

A homeowner cannot always tell a stock photo from a real one. What they can tell is that something feels generic. You already own the fix and it costs nothing — the real photographs simply need to replace the bought ones.

Nothing on the site measures anything.

Three navigation links still point at theme demo pages.

The menu contains items pointing to ?page_id=48, ?page_id=59 and /project-02-columns/. Those are leftover pages from the website template, never removed and never renamed, and two of them show raw page numbers instead of a proper address.

Two more are simply wrong: Privacy Policy points at the contact page, and Terms and Conditions points at the about page. Neither document appears to exist.

None of it stops the phone ringing. All of it tells anyone who looks closely that nobody has been through the site properly since it went up.

Not one review or accreditation appears anywhere on the site.

The whole site was searched for the things homeowners look for: Checkatrade, TrustATrader, Trustpilot, Google reviews, NFRC, CompetentRoofer, TrustMark. None of them appear. The only trust language is the words "Insured" and "Guaranteed", neither backed by anything a visitor can check.

Roofing is a trust purchase. Someone comparing three roofers picks the one they can verify, and right now there is nothing on this site to verify. Whatever reputation exists offline is doing no work online.

Housekeeping On The Register

Both companies have overdue confirmation statements today.

Reddish Roofing Limited (10317721, incorporated August 2016) has a confirmation statement that fell due on 17 August. A second company, Reddish Roofing and Building Ltd, was incorporated in July 2025 at the same address and has never traded — its confirmation statement was due 1 August, and identity verification with it.

There is also history worth knowing: a first gazette notice for compulsory strike-off in March 2025, discontinued eleven days later once the late accounts went in, plus an amended set of accounts re-filed in January 2026.

None of this is distress, and there are no penalties, charges or insolvency anywhere on the record. It is the same admin-under-pressure pattern that shows up in the website housekeeping, and it is worth ten minutes with the accountant this week.

One To Look At Properly

The finance page has the branding but not the required wording.

Ideal4Finance is mentioned 21 times across the site, with three banner images uploaded. Your craft credentials are mentioned zero times.

The finance page itself carries marketing copy, a phone number and a form — but no APR, no representative example, no credit-broker or introducer status disclosure and no FCA reference number. Promoting third-party consumer credit normally requires that wording, and right now the branding has gone up without it.

This is worth raising with Ideal4Finance rather than ignoring. They will have compliant wording they want you to use, and it is a five-minute fix rather than a problem.

Smaller Things That Add Up

Four fixes that take an afternoon between them.

The email address is a Hotmail account. reddishroofing@hotmail.co.uk is the contact address, on a business that already owns reddishroofing.co.uk. A matching address on your own domain costs almost nothing and reads very differently on a quote.

There is no landline. A single mobile, 07950 398261, is the only number on the site. That is normal for a small firm, but it does mean there is no fallback when it is not answered, and no call tracking is possible on it as it stands.

Saturday is missing from the opening hours. The site lists weekdays 8.30am to 6.00pm and Sunday closed. Saturday simply is not mentioned, so a homeowner with a weekend leak has no idea whether to ring.

The finance option is buried. There is a finance page and a lender banner, but finance is not mentioned beside any service or price. For an unplanned roof bill, a monthly figure next to a total is one of the strongest levers available, and right now the total is the only number anyone sees.
